# FLIGHT MANUAL :: OOAK INSTRUMENTS
*by OOAKOSIMO — hand-coded instruments for projection, audiences, and time.*

---

## WHAT THIS IS
Each artwork is **one hand-coded HTML file**. No app, no install, no account, no platform.
It runs in any browser — including the browser inside the small modified projector it can
live on. The projection mapping happens **inside the artwork itself**. The remote control
is **a webpage on your phone**. Everything here is deliberately simple enough to outlive
every platform it touches.

## THE CONTROLS

**Computer** — open the page. `S` settings panel · `P` pin/mapping mode · `F` fullscreen ·
`M` mic reactivity · `0` HUD. Drag a photo onto ChonkParade to feed the melt.

**Phone (the CONTROL ROOM)** — scan the QR or open the console page. Invent a pilot
nickname, enter the **room word**, take the controls. Sliders move light across the room.
**⚡ FLASH MY NAME** puts your pilot name on the projection for a moment. Multiple pilots:
last touch wins; the wall shows who's flying. Dark/light theme toggle up top.

**Curator deck** — inside the console, behind a pass word: corner-select + trackpad for
nudging the mapping from a phone, projection size bigger/smaller, surface ratio, and a
confirm-to-reset. The pass is a courtesy gate, not a vault — real mapping authority is
the `P` key on the device itself.

## MAPPING (deck mode)
Press `P`: four corner handles appear. Drag them until the artwork sits exactly on the
door, the stairs, the object. Corners save **on that device** and survive reboots.
`Reset mapping` returns to flat centered — so every piece can always be shown clean,
or re-mapped by whoever owns it next. Arrow keys nudge a selected corner
(hold Shift for big steps).

## ROOMS & PAIRING
The console and the artwork meet on a **room word** — like tuning two radios to one
frequency. The exhibition runs on its own room; **each sold edition ships tuned to its
own room** (its edition serial), printed in its manual and QR. One universal console
flies them all; no edition can accidentally control another. Two collectors who *choose*
the same room word will find their pieces synchronized — consider that an easter egg.

## PRIVACY, HONESTLY
When you move a slider, the console sends a tiny message — your invented nickname plus
the value — through a public message relay to the artwork. That is the entire data flow.

- **Sent:** invented nickname + control values. Nothing else.
- **Never asked for:** your real name, number, location, camera, mic, contacts, cookies.
- **Stored by the artist:** nothing. No server, no database, no analytics, no tracking.
- **Stored on your phone:** just your nickname, in your own browser, so the page
  remembers you.
- **Fine print:** the relay is a shared public service (think: an open radio band).
  Messages travel encrypted in transit, but anyone who knows the room word could listen
  or play too — and since the messages are only nicknames and slider values, the worst
  case is *more people playing the artwork*, which is arguably the artwork working.
  The pages load two open-source libraries from public CDNs, same as any website.
- Most "interactive" gallery tech routes you through commercial apps that harvest data
  as the price of participation. This piece is the opposite: the artwork is a text
  file, participation is a webpage, and the only thing collected is joy.

## EDITIONS
- **Soft edition** — the signed HTML file (edition number + collector name written into
  the code header), a permanent hosted URL, and this manual.
- **USB edition** — the above on a physical USB with the printed manual.
- **Deluxe edition** — the modified projector itself: piece pre-loaded, mapping
  pre-taught, obeying its own room. The projector is the sculpture.
- A soft-copy sibling of a work may also exist as a mint (Tezos/Ethereum); the signed
  file is the edition of record and the mint references it.
- Copying: the code is visible by nature — that's net art. Copies are how the work
  travels; **editions are how authorship is collected.** Websites sold as artworks
  follow a lineage running through Rafaël Rozendaal. *(License line: draft — the

## LINEAGE
The walking creatures descend from the artist's own shadow practice, begun 2017,
first published as the video work *shadow realm* (2020, YouTube/Rarible): *"to be able
to safely challenge yourself in these more advanced, demanding arenas, you have to,
absolutely have to, know your own energy."* The shadow-figure theme owes a friendly nod
to the artist **Marko Zubak, a.k.a. Mlibty** — acknowledged in every early mint, publicly
welcomed by him, and personally blessed by him for this exhibition and its editions.
The word "chonk" arrived through the Chonkly community, where several of these works
were first collected. The door piece exists because a friend dared her. The stick
walker is the creature becoming fully its own. Influences are gifts, not debts; all of
them are named here once, with love, and the receipts are on-chain.

## TROUBLESHOOTING
- **Blank projection:** check the device's internet and the URL; reload from the launcher.
- **Console not responding:** both ends need internet; confirm the *same room word*;
  reconnect with TAKE THE CONTROLS.
- **Mapping looks wrong:** someone moved the projector — physical position first, then
  re-pin. Mapping is per-device and saved.
- **Worst case on the projector:** Settings → Factory Reset restores everything.
  Nothing in this system touches firmware.
